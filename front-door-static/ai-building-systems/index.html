History: the Iran–Contra affair (1985–1987)

In 1985 and 1986, senior officials in President Ronald Reagan’s administration secretly arranged sales of U.S. weapons to Iran, a country then under a U.S. arms embargo, in part to win the release of American hostages held in Lebanon.

Part of the money from those sales was diverted to the Contra rebels fighting Nicaragua’s Sandinista government, even though the Boland Amendment had cut off U.S. funding for the Contras.

National Security Council aide Lt. Col. Oliver North and National Security Adviser John Poindexter were central figures; investigators also examined the role of CIA Director William Casey, who died in May 1987 before that question was resolved.

The Tower Commission, a review board Reagan appointed, delivered its report on February 26, 1987. Independent Counsel Lawrence Walsh, appointed in December 1986, charged 14 people and won 11 convictions, but the convictions of North and Poindexter were overturned on appeal. On December 24, 1992, President George H. W. Bush pardoned six people, including former Defense Secretary Caspar Weinberger, who had not yet gone to trial.

On the separate drug question, the 1989 report of the Senate’s Kerry Committee found substantial evidence of drug smuggling by individual Contras, Contra suppliers, pilots and supporters, and found that the State Department had paid some Contra-supply companies tied to drug traffickers, but it did not find that Contra leaders were personally involved. The CIA Inspector General’s 1998 report found no information that the CIA as an organization or its employees conspired with or assisted Contra-related drug trafficking, while acknowledging that the agency had worked with some people who faced trafficking allegations.
