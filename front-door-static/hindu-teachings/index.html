The Trimurti · Three Forms of the Divine

In much of later Hindu thought the Trimurti names three aspects of supreme divinity working as one cycle: Brahma creates, Vishnu preserves, Shiva transforms (often called “the Destroyer”). Below we introduce each with S1R1US art and a short Wikipedia cite — then return to Shiva’s astra lore, Vasuki, and Samudra Manthana.

Brahma · The Creator

Brahma (Sanskrit: ब्रह्मा) is remembered as the Creator within the Trimurti — associated with creation, knowledge, and the Vedas. Classic iconography shows four heads (the four Vedas / four directions), four arms holding symbols of knowledge (Vedas, japamālā, kamandalu, lotus), seated on a lotus; his mount is the haṃsa (swan). He is linked with Prajapati and with the astra lore of the Brahmastra. Few temples in India are dedicated primarily to him; the most famous is Pushkar.

“Brahma (Sanskrit: ब्रह्मा, IAST: Brahmā) is a Hindu god, referred to as ‘the Creator’ within the Trimurti, the trinity of supreme divinity that includes Vishnu and Shiva.”

Source: Wikipedia · Brahma ↗ · Trimurti ↗ · Brahmastra ↗

Vishnu · The Preserver

Vishnu (Sanskrit: विष्णु — “all-pervasive”) is the Preserver within the Trimurti — restorer of dharma when the world tilts toward chaos. Iconography often shows dark-blue skin and four arms with the śaṅkha (conch), sudarśana cakra, gadā (mace), and padma (lotus). He descends as avatars (Daśāvatāra) — among them Rama and Krishna — to protect the good. Consort Lakshmi; mounts include Garuda; often shown on the serpent Śeṣa over the ocean of milk.

“Vishnu is known as The Preserver within the Trimurti, the triple deity of supreme divinity that includes Brahma and Shiva.”

Source: Wikipedia · Vishnu ↗ · Trimurti ↗ · Dashavatara ↗

Shiva · The Transformer (Destroyer)

In Shaivism, Shiva is the Supreme Being who creates, protects, and transforms the universe. In the Trimurti he is remembered as the transformer — dissolving what must end so renewal can begin. Classic iconography includes the trishula (trident), the damaru drum, the third eye, the Tripundra (three sacred lines of ash), the crescent moon, and the Ganges flowing through matted hair. He is Adiyogi — the first yogi — patron of yoga, meditation, and the arts, abiding on Mount Kailasa; householder with Parvati and children Ganesha and Kartikeya; complementary to Shakti / Devi. The lingam is his aniconic form. The Abhaya mudra signals fearlessness and protection. Below: our artistic vision of Shiva with the Brahmastra launching.

“Shiva is known as The Destroyer within the Trimurti, the Hindu trinity which also includes Brahma and Vishnu.”

Brahmastra · Astra of Brahma

The Brahmastra (Sanskrit: ब्रह्मास्त्र, Brahmāstra — “astra of Brahma”) is the supreme Hindu celestial weapon created and owned by the god Brahma, with a still more fearsome variant, the Brahmashirastra. Epic lore names only a handful of gods and warrior-heroes who could invoke it. On this page we show a S1R1US artistic vision of Shiva with that weapon launching — power held with restraint — beside the classic trishula.

“The Brahmastra (Sanskrit: ब्रह्मास्त्र, romanized: Brahmāstra, lit. 'Astra of Brahma') is the supreme Hindu celestial weapon (astra) created and owned by the god Brahma along with its more powerful variant, the Brahmashirastra.”

Source: Wikipedia · Brahmastra ↗ · Brahma ↗ · Trimurti ↗ · Vishnu ↗ · Shiva ↗

Vasuki Nagaraja Serpent King

Vasuki is Nagaraja — king of the nagas — often shown coiling around Shiva’s neck as a blessed ornament, gem (Nāgamani) upon his head. In Mahabharata lore, Bhima reaches Nagaloka and receives a divine drink from Vasuki that grants enormous strength. In Buddhist tradition, Vasuki and other naga kings appear among those who hear the Buddha’s sermons, protecting and honouring enlightened beings.

“In Hindu iconography, he is generally depicted coiling around the neck of Shiva, who is believed to have blessed and worn him as an ornament.”

Source: Wikipedia · Vasuki ↗

Samudra Manthana Churning of the Ocean

In the great churning of the Ocean of Milk, Vasuki becomes the living rope around Mount Mandara so that the gods and asuras can draw forth amrita — the nectar of immortality. Poison rises too; Shiva holds it in his throat (Nīlakaṇṭha). The myth is a map of transformation: courage, cooperation, and the willingness to metabolize what would destroy others.

“He is described to have allowed both the devas and the asuras to bind him to Mount Mandara, so that they could use him as their churning rope to extract the amrita from the Ocean of Milk.”
