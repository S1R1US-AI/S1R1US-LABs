What happens when governments overreach

The five stories below are what citizens around the world lived through in 2025 and 2026 when governments reached for more control: internet shutdowns, social media bans, blocked accounts, mass arrests and, in India, live AI facial recognition. We report them neutrally, with figures as published by the cited outlets, because facts are the front line of a peaceful defence. Read the sources and decide for yourself.

#1

Iran: nationwide protests and crackdown, December 2025 – February 2026

  • Scale: the rights group HRANA documented protests in 203 cities across all 31 provinces, on 682 occasions, in the 50 days after they began on 28 December 2025.
  • Arrests: HRANA recorded at least 53,777 arrests in that period.
  • Deaths: estimates vary widely. The government acknowledged more than 3,000 deaths (the Guardian). HRANA confirmed 7,007 deaths, with 11,744 more cases under review. A network of doctors told the Guardian the toll could exceed 30,000.
  • Control: a nationwide internet shutdown at the height of the protests made independent verification very difficult.

Sources

  • AP News: At least 6,221 people killed in Iran's crackdown on protests ↗
  • The Guardian: Disappeared bodies, mass burials and '30,000 dead': what is the truth of Iran's death toll? ↗
  • HRANA: The Crimson Winter: A 50 Day Record of Iran's 2025–2026 Nationwide Protests ↗

#2

Turkey: mass protests over the jailing of Istanbul's mayor, March 2025

  • Trigger: the detention and jailing of Istanbul mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu, President Erdoğan's main rival, on corruption charges, which he denies.
  • Scale: Reuters and the Guardian reported hundreds of thousands at the 29 March rally, the largest demonstrations in Turkey in more than a decade. The opposition CHP leader claimed 2.2 million attended; that figure is not independently verified.
  • Arrests: about 1,900 people were detained within 10 days, according to the interior minister.
  • Control: X said it objected to court orders from Turkey's communications regulator to block more than 700 accounts of news organisations, journalists, political figures and students.

Sources

  • Reuters: Large crowds rally in Istanbul over jailing of Erdogan's main rival ↗
  • The Guardian: Turkish opposition rallies in defence of jailed Istanbul mayor in mass protest ↗
  • The Guardian: Huge protests as Istanbul mayor jailed on day of likely presidential nomination ↗

#3

Nepal: Gen Z protest over a social media ban and corruption, September 2025

  • Trigger: the government blocked 26 unregistered social media platforms, including Facebook, YouTube, Instagram and WhatsApp, on top of long-running anger over corruption.
  • Deaths: at least 19 people were killed on 8 September. Nepal's Health Ministry later raised the toll to 72, with at least 2,113 injured.
  • Outcome: the ban was lifted, and Prime Minister K.P. Sharma Oli resigned on 9 September.

Sources

  • Reuters: Nineteen killed in Nepal in 'Gen Z' protest over social media ban, corruption ↗
  • Reuters: Death toll from Nepal's anti-corruption protests raised to 72 ↗
  • Al Jazeera: Nineteen killed in Nepal protests against corruption and internet bans ↗

#4

India: Cockroach Janta Party protests and AI facial recognition, July – October 2026

  • Summer: a youth-led movement, the Cockroach Janta Party, protested for five weeks over exam paper leaks. Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on 25 July 2026.
  • AI surveillance: RSF reported real-time facial recognition, CCTV with AI, mobile surveillance vans, mobile internet suspension around the protest site and removal of journalists' posts during the July protests.
  • October: the movement returned over the update of the voter rolls, which has removed at least 130 million names so far. The Election Commission says it is a routine exercise. Media reports said about 7,000 protesters were detained and released on 10 October, with hundreds more detained on 11 October, while internet services in central Delhi were curtailed.
  • Courts: on 11 October India's Supreme Court ordered internet and transport restored and said there should be no illegal detention.

Sources

  • BBC News: India's 'cockroach' protest called off after education minister quits ↗
  • BBC News: Delhi sees more protests as freed 'cockroach' group's leaders vow to fight on ↗
  • RSF: India: Suppression of the July student protest coverage remains in effect ↗

#5

United Kingdom: the digital ID petition, 2025 – 2026

  • Not a street uprising: this was a petition, but it was directly about surveillance. Petitioners called a national digital ID "a step towards mass surveillance and digital control".
  • Scale: the petition "Do not introduce Digital ID cards" closed on 9 January 2026 with 2,984,191 signatures, according to the official UK Parliament petitions site. TechCrunch reported that only one parliamentary petition on record has drawn more signatures. Parliament debated it on 8 December 2025.
  • Outcome: in July 2026 the new government of Prime Minister Andy Burnham confirmed it was scrapping the digital ID card scheme.

Sources

  • TechCrunch: UK government scraps plans for digital ID cards after millions of Brits opposed ↗
  • UK Parliament Petitions: Do not introduce Digital ID cards ↗

What about protests against AI itself?

Marches against AI itself have been small so far. MIT Technology Review counted a couple of hundred people at a Pause AI and Pull the Plug march in London's King's Cross on 28 February 2026. The Daily Californian reported about 350 people at a peaceful march past OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind in San Francisco on 11 July 2026, which it called the largest anti-AI demonstration in American history.

Global Uprisings over financial inequality and surveillance grids

Six official news reports from 2024 to 2026 on mass protests over taxes, the cost of living, public money, corruption and surveillance. Each story links to the outlet. Headlines are quoted exactly; summaries are our own neutral words, with figures as reported. We critique systems, not people, and we never pick a side. Read the source and decide for yourself.

  1. India Election rolls · detentions · mobile internet cut

    India: AFP on the “cockroach” movement and the voter-roll protest

    AFP (Agence France-Presse) · 10 October 2026 · Arunabh SAIKIA

    India detains 'cockroach' movement leaders in bid to thwart election protest

    Indian police detained the head of the satirical "cockroach" online movement on Saturday as hundreds of protesters demanding the election commissioner's resignation gathered in New Delhi despite a heavy security crackdown.

    AFP · Arunabh SAIKIA · 10 October 2026 · via News24, credited to AFP ↗ · © Agence France-Presse

    AFP reported that the protest followed weeks of allegations of misconduct in a nationwide voter-roll revision, which the Election Commission of India rejects, saying the revision removes duplicate and ineligible voters. Authorities closed dozens of metro stations and temporarily shut down mobile internet around the protest site.

    Source: AFP via News24: India detains 'cockroach' movement leaders in bid to thwart election protest ↗ · byline also shown on Barron's (AFP) ↗

  2. Kenya Tax rises · cost of living

    Several killed as Kenyan police open fire on anti-tax bill protesters

    Al Jazeera · 25 June 2024 · Al Jazeera and news agencies

    Thousands joined a youth-led march in Nairobi to demand that lawmakers reject a finance bill that would raise taxes during a cost-of-living crisis. After the bill passed, protesters breached the parliament complex and police fired live rounds. The Kenya Medical Association and other groups reported at least five people killed and 31 wounded that day.

    Source: Al Jazeera: Several killed as Kenyan police open fire on anti-tax bill protesters ↗

  3. Indonesia Lawmakers' allowances · austerity

    Protests erupt in Indonesia over privileges for parliament members and ‘corrupt elites’

    The Guardian · 26 August 2025 · Kate Lamb

    Thousands of students, workers and activists protested outside parliament in Jakarta against a monthly housing allowance of 50 million rupiah for members of parliament, almost 10 times the Jakarta minimum wage, during a government austerity drive. Police used water cannon and teargas.

    Source: The Guardian: Protests erupt in Indonesia over privileges for parliament members and ‘corrupt elites’ ↗

  4. Philippines Flood-control funds · corruption

    Clashes, arrests as tens of thousands protest corruption in Philippines

    Al Jazeera · 21 September 2025 · Al Jazeera and news agencies

    Tens of thousands of people demonstrated in Manila over alleged corruption in public flood-control projects. The president had said in July that a majority of 9,855 projects worth more than $9.5bn showed anomalies, and set up an independent commission to investigate. Scattered clashes were reported, and local authorities said at least 17 people were arrested.

    Source: Al Jazeera: Clashes, arrests as tens of thousands protest corruption in Philippines ↗

  5. Serbia Public works · corruption · accountability

    Serbia's largest-ever rally sees 325,000 protest against government

    BBC News · 15 March 2025 · Guy De Launey

    Hundreds of thousands of people gathered in Belgrade over the deaths of 15 people when a renovated railway station canopy collapsed in Novi Sad. Student organisers asked for full transparency and accountability, and demonstrators blamed corruption and corner-cutting. An independent monitor estimated 275,000 to 325,000 people attended.

    Source: BBC News: Serbia's largest-ever rally sees 325,000 protest against government ↗

  6. Hungary Facial recognition · right to assemble

    Tens of thousands defy Hungary’s ban on Pride in protest against Orbán

    The Guardian · 28 June 2025 · Ashifa Kassam

    Tens of thousands of people marched in Budapest after the government banned the city's Pride event; organisers estimated 180,000 to 200,000. The government had said it would use facial recognition software to identify people attending banned events, with possible fines of up to €500, which prompted concerns in Hungary and beyond.

    Source: The Guardian: Tens of thousands defy Hungary’s ban on Pride in protest against Orbán ↗
