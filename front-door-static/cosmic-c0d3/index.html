Science The Mandelbrot set: a simple rule, infinite detail

Pick a complex number c. Start at zero and repeat one step: square it and add c (z → z² + c). If the numbers stay bounded forever, c belongs to the Mandelbrot set. Color every point by how fast it escapes, and you get the image at the top of this page.

  • Self-similarity: zoom into the edge and you find smaller copies of the whole shape, decorated differently, again and again.
  • Symmetry: the set is a perfect mirror image across the real axis, because the rule treats a number and its conjugate the same way.
  • Infinite boundary: its edge is so wrinkled that it is mathematically proven to have Hausdorff dimension 2 (Shishikura, 1998).
  • Nature's geometry: in The Fractal Geometry of Nature (1982), Benoit Mandelbrot showed that coastlines, clouds, trees and blood vessels repeat patterns across scales in a similar way.

Philosophy Quantum Mechanics as the undefined fabric

Science Quantum mechanics is the most precisely tested theory in physics. It describes matter and light as probabilities that become definite when measured. Physicists still do not agree on what that means, and quantum mechanics has not yet been joined with gravity and space-time.

Philosophy Somehow Quantum Mechanics has a layered fabric among our answers. We seek to define God, Gravity and Space Time, and we are open to god or gods or something undefined and much greater. We wonder whether reality feeds a cosmic construct that both records and simulates reality, and whether self-similar patterns like the Mandelbrot set hint at how that fabric is woven. Our guidance comes from Ancient Eastern philosophy: harmony, patience and balance.

We are getting closer to finding the TRUTH to humanity's MOST important questions.

  • Where did we come from? Honesty and truth about how we arrived on planet Earth.
  • What is our destiny? Most importantly, we want to understand it.
  • What orchestrates creation and destruction? The question behind every other question about our universe.

Test theory and philosophy with AI

You do not need a lab to start. Ask an AI to render a fractal, explain an equation, find the counter-argument to your favorite idea, or point you to the original papers. Then check the sources yourself. Good philosophy survives questions; good science survives tests.

  1. Ask: write your idea as one clear sentence.
  2. Separate: have AI mark what is proven, what is theory and what is belief.
  3. Test: run the math or simulation, and look for what would prove you wrong.
  4. Share: publish what you found, including what did not work.
